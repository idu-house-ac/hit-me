Logline

With their lives already falling apart, two brothers witness a murder and are forced to learn a deadly new job on the fly.

Overview

HIT ME is a grounded dark comedy about two brothers making the worst possible decisions in the worst possible situations.

As their worlds collapse around them, survival comes at a cost they may not be able to afford.

The pilot

Forty-nine pages, three acts

Act I: Two ordinary bad days.

Brandon sleeps in, dodges his girlfriend's questions and sells weed outside a high school for cash and shoelaces. Eric drags himself to an office where the only thing anyone remembers about him is a blocked layup.

Act II: The shortcut home.

Holly returns to the office. The brothers eat dinner at their mom's, drink too much and cut through the alley behind their building, where they watch Al-Pacino Brown shoot a man. He sees them too.

Act III: The job offer.

Holly corners Eric with an ultimatum while Brandon takes Eric's car to deliver food. Both end up at Alan's house, where Alan offers $150K to kill his wife. She overhears, attacks and dies by accident. The brothers follow Alan to an ATM.

Look and tone

HIT ME blends grounded realism with dark, situational comedy.

  1. Natural lighting and real-world locations create a relatable, lived-in feel.
  2. A mix of wide, static shots and handheld movement reflects control versus chaos.
  3. Dialogue-driven scenes allow humor and tension to coexist naturally.
  4. Violence is sudden and uncomfortable, never glamorized.

Why this story

We've all felt stuck, like life isn't moving the way it's supposed to.

HIT ME is about what happens when that pressure builds to a breaking point. These aren't criminals. They're regular people forced to adapt when life stops giving them options.

Directors' statement

HIT ME follows two brothers who are further behind than they expected to be. One is coasting and one is grinding, and neither is built for what happens to them. That is where the comedy lives: they talk when they should run, joke when they should think, and say the wrong thing to exactly the wrong person.

The script is blunt about race, sex, drugs and violence because its characters are. We play those moments for discomfort, not approval, and nobody comes out of them looking good. When violence arrives it is fast, clumsy and permanent.

The show is layered because people are, and it comes out of our own lives. Grief is in it, and so are ambition, loyalty and bad judgment. At its center is what happens when you lean out of comfort and into the unknown after a lifetime of playing it safe, and how the people closest to you adjust right along with you when you need them.

Brandon and Eric are not ready for any of it. They go anyway, and they go together.

Damon M. Smith and Aaron Caldwell

How the hard scenes get shot

  • No live firearms, no blanks

    Both shootings play as silhouette, sound and edit.

  • A coordinator on the knife and the falls

    Clean dialogue first, then rehearsed action with full resets.

  • Adults play the students

    Robby and Wes are written as 17 and cast at 18 or older.

  • The Holly scene is staged without contact

    Boundaries agreed in advance and shot on a closed set.
